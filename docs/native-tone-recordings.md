# Plan: native-speaker recordings for tone detection

Status: plan and recording kit written 2026-11-09. First native speaker: pass 1, blocks 1-13
(Parts A and B), recorded 2026-11-10; the rest (Parts C and D, pass 2) on 2026-11-13. Results
at the end.
Follows on from `tone-recognition.md`, which found that both prototypes (pitch shapes in
`tones.rs`, Whisper's tone scoring in `whisper::score`) are limited by the test voice, an old
synthetic voice that doesn't say its tones the way people do.

## What the recordings must answer

1. **Does the pitch tracker + tone shapes work on a real voice?** It got 45% on the synthetic
   voice. Measure on native single syllables (Part A) and words (Part B), untuned.
2. **Does personal calibration (option B) work?** Build templates from one pass of a speaker's
   Part A, classify the other pass. Same speaker, different day and speed: the learner's case.
3. **Does it generalise across speakers (option C)?** With 2+ speakers: tune on some, test on
   the one left out.
4. **Are wrong tones caught?** Part C has real words said with a wrong tone on purpose (lāoshī
   for 老师). This is exactly where Whisper fails: its word knowledge "hears" the right word. The
   number that matters is the **false-accept rate**: wrong-tone items a checker still passes.
5. **Does Whisper hear tones better with large-v3-turbo** than small did (23/40 single syllables)?

## The recording kit

Generated by `python scripts/tone_sheet.py` (seeded shuffle, so it's reproducible):

- `docs/tone-recordings/speaker-sheet.html`: the bilingual sheet the speaker reads and records
  from, with instructions, consent text and all blocks. Published (private) at
  https://claude.ai/artifact/1Nja6SkbLnzsHpv9DwMBgq.
- `docs/tone-recordings/items.tsv`: block, pass, position, item id, text, pinyin, tones as said,
  right tones. This is what maps each recorded block back to its items. **Don't re-run the
  script with a different seed or item list once someone has recorded.**

| Part | What | Items | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | 20 syllables × 4 tones (妈麻马骂 … 千钱浅欠), characters shown | 80 | Clean tone contrast. The first 10 sets match the synthetic test list, so old and new numbers compare directly. The other 10 add onsets the first set lacks (zh ch sh q h, zero onset, -n/-ng finals). |
| B | Two-syllable words: every tone pair 1-1 … 4-4 twice, 3-3 sandhi (你好 水果 可以), neutral tones (妈妈 谢谢 朋友 …), 不 and 一 changes | 47 | Tones in connected speech, sandhi labelled as said. |
| C | Real words said with one wrong tone, pinyin only (no characters, so the speaker isn't pulled back to the right tone) | 20 | The learner's mistake, said reliably. No 3-3 pairs, which sandhi would blur. |
| D | Short sentences, incl. a question | 10 | Intonation and phrase-level tone, for later. |

Two passes: **pass 1** (clear, teacher-like) has everything, 157 items in 16 blocks; **pass 2**
(everyday speed, other day if possible) repeats A and B in a new order, 127 items in 13
blocks. 284 clips, about 40 minutes of the speaker's time. Within a block, no two syllables of
the same Part A set are adjacent, so the speaker isn't reading mā má mǎ mà as a drill.

Each block is one phone recording: say "第N组", pause, then each item with ~2 s gaps. A
mistake means re-recording the block (a minute), never repeating inside it, so the cutter can
count segments. The pinyin is coloured with the app's tone colours.

## Speakers

- **Aim for 2–4**, mixed female and male (pitch range matters most for the tracker and the
  speaker normalisation), at least one with standard northern Mandarin. A southern or Taiwan
  speaker is useful later as a harder case (lower 3rd tone, fewer neutral tones), but label it.
- A paid online tutor (italki, Preply) for an hour is a straightforward way to get one quickly;
  friends work too. The sheet collects gender, age range, where they grew up, other
  dialects, phone, and two consent choices (develop/test; train a shipped model).
- **If the speaker is in mainland China**, claude.ai and Google Fonts are blocked there: send the
  HTML file itself (`docs/tone-recordings/speaker-sheet.html`, opens in any browser and falls
  back to system Chinese fonts) or print it to PDF, instead of the link.
- The link is private until you share it from the page's Share menu.

## When the recordings arrive

Keep audio out of git: put it under `tone-data/<speaker>/` (ignored) and only commit code.

1. **Cut** (`scripts/tone_cut.py`): ffmpeg each file to 16 kHz mono; energy-based
   voice detection on 10 ms frames (threshold from the file's own noise floor); split at
   silences ≥ 600 ms; drop blips < 120 ms. Expect 1 + k segments (the "第N组" announcement plus
   the block's items). Read the block number from the announcement with Whisper, check it
   against the file order, and **list every block whose segment count is off** to fix by hand
   or ask for again, rather than guessing.
2. **Write the bench format** per speaker and pass: `clean/N.wav` (16-bit mono, written with
   Python's `wave` so the header is exactly 44 bytes; the benches skip 44 bytes blindly, and
   ffmpeg can add a LIST chunk), 150 ms padding each side, peak-normalised; `noisy/N.wav` mixed
   with room noise at ~10 dB SNR, like the synthetic set; `list.tsv` in canonical order (A, B,
   D, C) with the tones as said; and a `meta.tsv` with id, part, pass and right tones (the
   benches split `list.tsv` on the first tab and read every byte after it as a tone, so extra
   columns must go in a separate file).
3. **Bench changes** (small): `whisper_tone_bench` takes the first 40 lines as ten tone sets;
   read the sets from `meta.tsv` (Part A, groups of 4) so all 20 count. Report per part; for
   Part C report false accepts (pitch: classified tone equals the right tone on the changed
   syllable; Whisper: the target word outscores the tone distractors). Add a
   leave-one-speaker-out mode to `tone_bench`, and a personal-template mode (templates from
   pass 1 Part A, classify pass 2).
4. **Re-run** `tone_bench` (untuned shapes, then tuned) and `whisper_tone_bench` with small and
   large-v3-turbo on every speaker.

## Reading the results

Suggested thresholds, to adjust once real numbers exist:

- **Untuned pitch ≥ 85% on Part A** (pass 1): the tracker and shapes are sound; build option A
  (pitch graph) and B (personal templates) straight away.
- **60–85%**: tune the shapes or the classifier weight on one speaker and test on another
  before trusting it; look at the confusion matrix (2↔3 is the classic one).
- **Personal templates on pass 2 ≥ 90%**: option B is enough, and a trained classifier (C) can
  wait.
- **Part C false accepts**: expect Whisper to pass most of them. If pitch catches ≥ 80% while
  passing ≥ 90% of right-tone words, option D (grade only when they agree, otherwise "not sure")
  is worth building.

## Later: the learner's side

Native recordings only show native tones. The next step is the learner (you) recording the
same sheet's Parts A, B and D, then a native speaker **labelling** what tone they actually hear
in each syllable. That gives real learner errors with a trustworthy label, which is the test
that decides whether a "wrong tone" verdict can be shown to a learner. A small labelling page
(play clip, tap the tone heard) would make that quick.

## First results (2026-11-10)

Speaker 1 (native, male, about 128 Hz, ±9 st), pass 1, blocks 1-13: Parts A and B. Blocks
14-16 (Part C wrong tones, Part D sentences) and pass 2 still to come. Phone recordings (ALAC,
44.1 kHz) with the gaps gated to digital silence. `python scripts/tone_cut.py
docs/tone-recordings/recordings tone-data/speaker1` found the announcement plus every item in
all 13 blocks: 127 clips.

`SPEAKER_DIR=../tone-data/speaker1 cargo test --release --lib -- --ignored --nocapture
tones::tests::speaker_bench` (the app's classifier; syllables judged right, neutral tones
not judged, sandhi counted as said):

| | Part A (alone) | Part B (in words) | Tone Perfect (6 speakers, alone) |
|---|---|---|---|
| before, clean | 74/80, 92% | 52/85, 61% (+3 no pitch) | 89.7% |
| before, noisy | 67/80, 84% | 47/84, 56% (+4 no pitch) | 87.4% |
| after, clean | 74/80, 92% | **66/88, 75%** | **90.8%** |
| after, noisy | 67/80, 84% | **60/88, 68%** | **91.3%** |

What was wrong, and the fixes (in `tones.rs`):

1. **Creaky syllables disappeared.** This speaker's low stretches (3rd tones, the ends of 4th
   tones, low 2nd tones) are often creak: loud, but with no pitch YIN can follow. Only 77% of
   loud frames got a pitch. Syllables were found from pitched frames only, so in kǎo-shì,
   hǎo-kàn and tiào-wǔ the creaky syllable vanished and the other one was judged in its place.
   Now `syllables` finds syllables from pitched frames *and* creak, and `contour` counts creak
   inside a syllable (4+ frames) as the bottom of the voice, as it already did for a creaky
   tail. The creak level is now also at least 3× the background, so noise doesn't count as
   voice. This alone took Part B from 52 to 59 right, and helped Tone Perfect (noisy +3.9).
2. **No dipping 3rd tone before another syllable.** Before another syllable a 3rd tone is the
   low half third (21), never 214, but the 214 shape was allowed everywhere and it matched
   low 2nd tones that dip slightly before rising (zuó-tiān, míng-tiān): 59 → 63.
3. **Height counts half in words** (`WORD_LEVEL` 0.5). Pitch drifts down through a phrase,
   so this speaker's 2nd tones in words start at the bottom of the voice and rise only to the
   middle (Zhōng-guó, Měi-guó, xīn-wén): by height they look like 3rd tones. 63 → 66.
   0.3 scored 69 but sits next to a cliff (0.2: 60, 3rd tones collapse), so 0.5 until
   another speaker can confirm.

Tried and dropped: a looser YIN (`YIN_GLOBAL` 0.6: more pitch found, but wrong pitch, Part A
92% → 79%); a 500 Hz low-pass (Part B +6 but noisy Part A −2 and noisy Part B −2); moving
short pitch stretches that jump an octave away and back (no change here, slightly worse on
Tone Perfect).

Still missed: 2nd tones in words (29/44 right; 15 heard as 3rd), a few octave errors where
the voice turns creaky between syllables (qiān-bǐ, shēn-tǐ heard 13→14), and 4th tones that
fall only a little in words. Single syllables were already at 92% and didn't change.
`SPEAKER_SHAPES=2` prints every tone-2 contour; `SPEAKER_RUNS=1` how each word was split.
Check any further change on a second speaker and on Tone Perfect before keeping it.

### What the new recordings show

1. **Syllables said alone hold up at everyday speed** (90% on both passes), with this
   speaker's usual syllable a fifth shorter in pass 2 (33 frames against 41).
2. **Words get worse at everyday speed**: 73% to 66%. Nearly all of it is 2nd tones:
   in words they were right 15 times of 40 in pass 1 and 9 of 24 in pass 2.
3. **2nd tone heard as 3rd is the one large error**: 21 of 61 in pass 1, 21 of 44 in pass 2,
   alone as well as in words (chuáng, fáng, bá, má are missed in both passes). This
   speaker's 2nd tone sits at the bottom of the voice and rises only at the very end, or,
   inside a word at everyday speed, hardly at all.
4. **4th tone heard as 3rd is the second**: 19 of 70 in pass 1, in words. After another
   syllable a 4th tone starts in the middle of the voice and falls into creak, which is what
   a low 3rd tone looks like.
5. **Sentences (60%) fail earlier than the tones**: with no pause between syllables, cutting
   the pitch into as many pieces as there are syllables puts the cuts in the wrong places
   (pieces 1 to 12 frames long, syllables with no pitch at all). The tone shapes never get a
   fair look.

### Part C can't be scored yet

The wrong tones were passed as the right ones 3 times of 20, which looks good, but the
recordings don't support a figure. Of the eight syllables the sheet asked for as a 2nd tone
(shēn-**tí**, **wén**-tí, **qí**-chē, **dián**-nǎo, kǎo-**shí**, fēi-**jí** …), seven have no rise in the
recording: the pitch is level and high, or falls (`late_rise_bench` prints each one). The
tracker is not in doubt there: the pitch is steady and clean. The speaker, reading pinyin for
words that don't exist, did not say the tone that was asked for. The 1st and 4th tones asked
for were said (lāo-shī, hǎo-kān, tiāo-wǔ and lǚ-xìng are heard as written).

So the label "tone as said" is wrong for an unknown part of Part C, and neither the 50% nor
the 3 of 20 means what it seems to. Before Part C is used, someone who hears tones has to
listen to the 20 clips and write down what was said (the labelling page proposed above under
"Later: the learner's side" would do for this too). I can't listen to them.

### One thing tried: counting a late rise

`late_rise_bench`: in words, the further the last fifth of a syllable climbs above its
lowest earlier point, the more it counts for a 2nd tone and against a 3rd.

| | pass 1, Part B | pass 1, 2nd tones in words | pass 2, Part B | pass 2, 2nd tones in words |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| as shipped | 64/88 | 15/40 | 57/87 | 9/24 |
| late rise, strongest setting | 67/88 | 20/40 | 57/87 | 10/24 |

It helps the careful pass and does nothing at everyday speed, where the 2nd tones barely
rise. Not adopted: it would be fitted to one speaker reading carefully.

### Where improvement could come from

- **A second and third speaker.** Everything about words rests on one man's voice, and his
  low, late 2nd tone is the main error. Whether that is him or Mandarin can't be told from
  one speaker. This is still the most useful thing to get.
- **A model for syllables in words.** The learned model (2026-11-13) only knows syllables said
  alone, and made words worse. Words need their own, with the neighbouring syllables' pitch
  as inputs (a 2nd tone is low *against what follows it*, a 3rd is low throughout). Tone
  Perfect has no words, so this needs word recordings from several speakers: pass 1 and
  pass 2 here are 175 syllables from one.
- **Creak as a cue between 2nd and 3rd.** Still untried. In a syllable said alone, this
  speaker's 3rd tones have stretches with no pitch and his 2nd tones are pitched throughout;
  7 single 2nd tones are heard as 3rd in each pass.
- **Finding syllables in running speech.** Whisper's word timings (or a forced alignment) to
  place the cuts, instead of dividing the pitch track by the number of syllables. Needed
  before sentences can be judged at all.
- **Labels for Part C**, as above, to get the number that matters most for telling a learner
  "that was the wrong tone": how often a wrong tone is passed.
